RW-5 Ferret Rotorwheel

A hybrid ground–air scout: four road wheels with ducted lift fans in their hubless centers, plus a tilting tail fan that boosts liftoff and pushes in cruise. Doctrine in one line: ground for endurance, air for obstacles.

01Concept & mission profile

Wheel-sized rotors are aerodynamically poor — that constraint is examined honestly in §06 — so the RW-5 is not a drone that also drives. It is a ground scout that can jump: over rubble, wire, gaps, walls, stairwells, between floors and rooftops, then resume driving. The airframe is sized by the single most binding requirement for interior work: it must pass a standard doorway under its own power in drive mode.

The design mission splits roughly 90% ground / 10% air by distance. Flight is a budget of short hops (≤ 12 min total, ~3–4 km cumulative), spent only where wheels fail. The quiet mode is the ground mode: fly briefly, land beyond line-of-sight, approach on wheels.

02Configuration

  • Layout — 4 rotor-wheel modules on tilting swing-arms, skid-steered on the ground; 1 tail fan on a 0–90° tilting pylon.
  • Drive mode — wheel planes vertical, fans stopped, tail pylon folded flat into the deck.
  • Flight mode — swing-arms tilt each wheel 95° outboard so all four fan discs are horizontal; tail pylon vertical for liftoff, tilting aft for translational flight.
  • Hover control — quad differential thrust; tail fan carries ~15% at liftoff and trims pitch. Ground control — independent per-wheel torque, zero-radius turns, regenerative braking.

03Rotor-wheel module

The wheel is hubless: an open-center structural rim carries everything and leaves the center free for the fan. From outside in: an airless shear-band tire ring (Ø 520 mm, no blowouts); a rim-drive ring motor for the ground mode (direct drive, no gearbox, 1.4 kW continuous); the structural rim, which doubles as the duct outer wall; a second, independent ring motor driving the 7-blade Ø 420 mm fan at its blade tips; and debris mesh on both duct faces (~5% thrust penalty, rejects objects over 8 mm).

Failure isolation: the two drivetrains share nothing but the rim. A wheel with a dead fan still drives; a wheel with a dead rim motor still lifts; land-and-drive is the universal abort.

Tail unit

A single fixed rear lift rotor — the naive version of this idea — does not work: mounted behind the center of gravity it produces an uncommanded pitch-down moment it cannot trim away, and it is dead weight in cruise. The RW-5 instead mounts its Ø 360 mm tail fan on a tilting pylon: vertical at liftoff, where its ~15% thrust share lets the CG sit slightly aft and gives direct pitch trim during ground-effect departure; tilted aft in cruise, where it becomes the pusher and lets the wheel fans unload toward lift-only — a compound layout, more efficient than dragging the airframe around on tilted lift fans.

06The hard problems

An honest concept spec earns its keep here. Five things are genuinely wrong with the wheel-rotor idea, and the design either answers them or prices them in.

6.1 — Disc loading is the tax on the whole idea

Total disc area is 0.66 m², so disc loading is ≈ 64 kg/m² — roughly 4× a conventional quadcopter of this mass. Ideal hover power T1.5/√(2ρA) ≈ 6.6 kW; at a figure of merit of 0.65 (small ducted fans plus mesh losses), ≈ 11 kW at hover. There is no mitigation, only accounting: flight must be treated as a sprint. Any mission needing more than ~15 minutes airborne belongs on a different airframe.

6.2 — Tilt mechanisms are the reliability budget

Four swing-arm actuators, four hubless rims, eight ring motors. The simpler alternative — fixed horizontal lift fans buried in the body — was rejected because it either widens the vehicle past doorway width or shrinks the fans further, which is worse than 6.1. Mitigations: mechanical endpoint locks (unpowered holding in both modes), full drive capability with any tilt actuator failed.

6.3 — Mud is worse than debris

Mesh stops stones; wet mud caking the mesh and duct is the real thrust killer. Accepted limitation: after deep-mud driving, VTOL is degraded until a spin-clean cycle (fans pulsed at ground idle) or manual clearing.

6.4 — Acoustics

64 kg/m² is loud — est. 85–90 dBA at 10 m in hover. Uneven blade spacing and duct liners shape the tone, not the level. The fix is doctrinal, not mechanical: the approach happens on wheels.

The rotorwheel buys ground endurance and silence at the price of complexity and flight time. If the mission is mostly air, build the cage. If it is mostly ground with vertical obstacles — the stated interior / rough-terrain case — the rotorwheel is defensible.

07Development path

  • Gen-0, bench — one rotor-wheel module on a thrust stand and dyno: validate FoM ≥ 0.6 with mesh, ring-motor thermals, tilt-lock loads.
  • Gen-0.5, surrogate — a COTS quadcopter bolted to a skid-steer chassis, flown against the 90/10 mission profile — proves the doctrine before paying for integration.
  • Gen-1 — full airframe: tethered hover, then free transitions.
  • Gen-2 — environmental hardening (mud, sand, EMI) and autonomous doorway / stairwell traversal.
