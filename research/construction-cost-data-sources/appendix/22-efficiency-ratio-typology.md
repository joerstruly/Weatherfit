**Methodological warning on REBNY:** REBNY loss factor is explicitly described in the sources as a *market-driven, artificial number* added onto usable area — it is a pricing convention, not a measurement of net÷gross. Do not treat 27% as an efficiency ratio. BOMA-measured load factor (rentable÷usable ≈ **1.10–1.15**) is the measured analogue and is a different, smaller number.

**Note on definitional mismatch:** these three families of numbers are *not* the same ratio. Pena's 65% for apartments is net-assignable÷gross. MFE's 70–75% is net-rentable÷gross. GSA's 80% is usable÷gross. CTBUH-family academic work is net÷GFA. Your model must pick one definition and hold it; mixing them is a bigger error source than the ratio's dispersion.

## Not found / dead ends — stop re-searching these

1. **BOMA Experience Exchange Report does NOT publish load factor or rentable/usable statistics.** It is an operating-expense and income benchmarking product, subscription-based at `eer.boma.org` (membership not required; pricing not published in search results). One result title — https://bomagt.org/downloads/Newsletters/talking_points_eer.pdf — reads "BOMA International has Suspended the…", suggesting the product may have been suspended at some point. **Dead end for efficiency.**
2. **BOMA Standard Methods of Measurement** is a paid standard that defines *how* to measure. It publishes no population statistics. Dead end for a ratio.
3. **GSA IOLP is a dead end for efficiency.** Confirmed field list is Location Code, Asset Name, Installation, Owned/Leased, Region, address, lat/long, **Rentable Square Feet, Available Square Feet, Construction Date** — there is **no gross square feet field**. https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/inventory-of-owned-and-leased-properties-iolp ; https://d2d.gsa.gov/report/gsa-inventory-owned-and-leased-properties-iolp
4. **PLUTO `ResArea` is NOT a net-rentable figure.** Its definition — "an estimate of the exterior dimensions of the portion of the structure(s) allocated for residential use," sourced from DOF's Property Tax System or CAMA — is a *gross* residential area. `ResArea ÷ BldgArea` measures **residential share of a mixed-use building, not efficiency.** Do not use it as an efficiency proxy. (PLUTO areas are also assessment-derived and known to be stale/noisy.)
5. **No US-specific, large-N, peer-reviewed multifamily net-to-gross study found.** The space-efficiency literature that exists (A1) is tall-building-focused and centered on Japan, Singapore, Tehran, and global supertalls. The one US-adjacent academic paper found, https://www.mdpi.com/2075-5309/11/3/99 (Univ. of Illinois, *Buildings* 2021, "Optimizing Multi-Family Building Massing…"), models cost/envelope trade-offs across height, building count, width and floor area for low-rise MF, and uses double-loaded corridors as its normalizing device — but the search summary surfaced no net-to-gross output values.
6. **REIT supplementals stating net rentable SF *and* gross SF for the same asset: NOT FOUND.** Apartment REITs disclose average home size and unit counts; office REITs disclose rentable SF. Neither discloses gross building area. The residential-REIT route yields unit size (valuable), not efficiency.
7. **Freddie Mac / Fannie Mae multifamily loan-level data: no net rentable SF found.** FHFA's Enterprise PUDB (https://www.fhfa.gov/data/pudb, https://www.fhfa.gov/data/datasets) carries property size and unit counts/affordability, not building SF pairs.
8. **NMHC does not publish an average-unit-size series of its own** beyond AHS-derived stock distributions.
9. **No state HFA cost study found that publishes a clean $/SF table by garden / podium / wrap / high-rise.** The closest are Abt's building-type categories and Minnesota's predictive-model variables — both of which require opening a PDF this session could not open.
10. **Terner Center is repeatedly conflated with contractor blogs in search results.** Several confident-sounding per-SF-by-construction-type numbers attributed to "Terner" in search summaries actually originate on terrapincg.com and apers.app. Verify attribution on every Terner number before use.

## The single best route to solving the efficiency-ratio problem

**Reparameterize the model so the efficiency ratio disappears as a free parameter.** Your identity is cost/unit = cost/SF × unit SF ÷ efficiency, which forces you to estimate the one quantity nobody publishes. But the product (unit SF ÷ efficiency) *is* just gross SF per unit — and gross SF per unit is directly and massively observable. Assessor and benchmarking data give you gross building area and residential unit count for hundreds of thousands of real US buildings, free and structured: NYC PLUTO (`BldgArea`, `UnitsRes`, plus `NumFloors` and `BldgClass` to stratify by typology — the third-party derivation of ~1,017 gross SF/unit citywide shows the calculation is trivially doable), Maryland's statewide CAMA on Socrata, Maricopa's Apartment Master, and the LL84-family energy-benchmarking disclosures in a dozen large cities. Estimate cost/GSF and GSF-per-unit directly, stratified by `NumFloors` bands that map to garden / podium / wrap / high-rise, and you never touch a 70–75% trade-magazine figure again. As a bonus, this single move also attacks risk #3, because `NumFloors` is the stratifier for both.

Keep the efficiency ratio only as a *reporting* output and a sanity check, computed by dividing published net unit size (UDR's 10-K average-home-size-by-market table is the best primary source, backed by Census SOC's median MF unit SF and RentCafe's city-level series) by your measured gross SF/unit. If that implied ratio lands in the 70–80% band, the two independent data families corroborate each other; if it doesn't, you have found a real definitional problem rather than inherited one. For the genuinely paired net-and-gross measurement — worth doing only for commercial, where the model needs office load factors — the one free at-scale path is joining SEC Form ABS-EE EX-102's per-property `netRentableSquareFeetNumber` to assessor gross building area by address; and for a defensible single anchor for office, GSA's PBS-P100 80% usable-to-gross design target beats any trade citation you currently hold.
